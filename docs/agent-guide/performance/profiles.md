# Profiles: hangs, hitches and hot functions

*`quern://guide/performance/profiles` -- part of `quern://guide/performance` in the Quern agent guide. Start from `quern://guide`.*

- Why the app froze or stuttered: `start_profile` (iOS: relaunches the app under Instruments, presets hangs, cpu, hitches, launch; Android: perfetto records without relaunching, presets hangs, frames, cpu -- its deep summary needs trace_processor, which quern downloads on first use and says so in the answer), drive the app, then `stop_profile` for hangs, hitches and hot functions -- on iOS with Xcode 27.2, the ten longest hangs say whether the main thread was `blocked`, `busy` or `mixed`, and the longest three give the stack it was in (the rest get none); one of those three that was blocked, in a profile without System Call Trace, has no stack, and its `stack_note` says why, and without triage `summary.unavailable.triage` says why; `download_profile` for the trace itself

A profile records for `time_limit_s` (30 s by default, up to 300) and finishes by itself at the limit, so give it long enough for the interaction; read a profile that ended that way with `get_profile`. On iOS the app launches slowly under Instruments: wait for its first screen (`wait_for_element`) before driving it, and relaunch it after the profile stops, since xctrace terminates it. `hitches` is physical devices only; simulators refuse Animation Hitches. `stop_profile` can take a minute or more while the trace is finished; past 4 minutes it answers 504 and the profile keeps finishing, so read it later with `get_profile`. A hang in the first few seconds can be the app's own cold launch. On Android 12 and later, `summary.frame_timeline` says which frames were janky and whose fault it was: the `hangs` and `frames` presets record it, `cpu` doesn't, and reading it needs trace_processor. `summary.unavailable.frame_timeline` says why when it's missing. An Android trace can also stop at perfetto's file limit before `time_limit_s` (`profiles.android_max_trace_mb` raises it, but never above `profiles.max_mb`: the smaller of the two is the limit, and when a trace ended early at that limit its `trace ended early` entry in `summary.trace_data_loss` names the setting): check `summary.trace_data_loss` and `summary.trace_note`, and read their absence as no loss detected, not as proof the trace is whole. They are only checked when trace_processor read the trace: if `summary.unavailable.trace_processor` is set, the check didn't run, and their absence says nothing. Kept profiles share `profiles.max_mb`, and the oldest finished ones make room.
